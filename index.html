Meghanath Macha

I build foundation models of consumer behavior, from where people go to what they do online.

At ZeroToOne.AI, I lead AI and platform. We train one shared model on physical and digital behavior, then use it to predict visits, intent and other outcomes. I started out training the models and still design the systems. Much of my work now is building the data, infrastructure and teams that support them.

Head of AI & Platform, ZeroToOne.AI

About

ZeroToOne.AI's Large Behavioral Model is a vertical foundation model: one model for one domain, serving every product instead of a model per task. It learns a representation of each ID (a device or other identifier) from its behavioral sequence, scores 1.7B+ IDs a day across 9,000+ behavioral categories, and refreshes every 24 hours.

I led the technical diligence for GroundTruth and WeatherBug and now lead the integration of the platforms and teams. Before that, I was a founding engineer on Adobe GenStudio, working on how generative AI could follow a company's brand guidelines.

I have a PhD from Carnegie Mellon, where I worked on privacy-preserving models of location data. Papers in ISR, KDD, DMKD and Management Science.

I live in California with my wife, daughter and our cat, Binxy.

Selected work

01

One model, many products

We used to train a separate classifier for each audience. Now the shared model is the base layer and the products sit on top of it: predictive audiences refreshed daily and delivered inside the ad platform, enrichment feeds of behavioral signals for a client's own data warehouse, and managed models for churn, customer value and next best action.

Outcomes from campaigns flow back into training.

02

Getting AI to follow brand rules

At Adobe GenStudio, I worked on grounding content generation in a knowledge graph of a company's brand guidelines. Verification ran as a separate step from generation, so the rules did not depend on which model wrote the content.

03

Audiences you can describe and check

Audience requests used to go through engineers. Now a planner describes who they want to reach, the agent turns that into an audience definition over the model's predictions, and the planner reviews what the system understood before using it.
